Agriculture & Horticulture Products
GROUND NUT

Termed commonly as 'Peanuts'. It is the favourite munchies of Man and Ape alike. Grown plentily in eastern and western part of Odisha. It is a nut with multiple applications which enable it to be eaten raw, cooked, as flour, as butter, as oil, and many more. Districts where the product is cultivate are Bolangir, Cuttack, Jajpur, Dhenkanal, Kendrapada, Mayurbhanj, Puri, Kalahandi, Sunderagrh etc.

PHULA BADI

The flower shaped crunchy delicacy is an essential element of Odia curries and has high demand in Odisha market. The soft and crispy item that is specially packaged to avoid damage is produced by over 200 women groups in Keonjhar District of Odisha.

KALAJEERA RICE

Kalajeera is Ethinic and traditional variety of ancient long grain white rice on aminiature scale - like baby Basmati, a black coloured small and oval grain variety. It is cultivated in the lowlands by the tribal farmers of koraput district.

SPICES

Turmeric is one of the spices which gives a great colour and texture to any kind of Indian Cuisine and works as a skincare. It is cultivated in an organic manner in the southern parts of Odisha called Haldi.

Ginger is an organic product cultivated in an organic environment which not only acts as an element iconic Indian Masala but also has its share in medicinal elements too.

Both Ginger and Turmeric provide a sustainable income to the tribal farmers.

Non Timber Forest Products
ROCK BEE HONEY

As its name suggests the honey is derived from the wild rock bees that create colonies under inaccessible rock cliffs and huge tree trunks. ORMAS has trained the primary honey collectors in the forest areas: on collection of honey and processing of same using low cost and simple techniques to retain its natural and medical values. It is found in the forest and hills of Mayurbhanj, Nuapada and other forest based districts of Odisha.

LEAF CUP PLATES

Nature's true gift in the shape of Siali leaves which gives us a truly eco-friendly disposable plate is found in the forest belts of Odisha. They are collected, stiched and dried under the sun, machine pressed with cardboard and lminated and are ready for sale. ORMAS has pioneered this product nationwise over the past 15 years.

BROOM

Hill Broom grass grows on hilly forest slopes of parts of Southern Odisha. It also serves as a major source of livelihood to the tribal poor of this region. The raw grass is properly harvested, collected, dried and stored. Trained by ORMAS, the tribals bind it in proper length and size and cap it with a user friendly plastic handle to make it acceptable in urban market, as a qualitative product.

Thorn Broom is used widely in outdoor cleaning purposes. This variant of broom is derived out of raw materials grown in western part of Odisha. The combination and stiffness of this broom makes it highly effective on rough surfaces.

Coco Broom(Stick Broom) is the most raw and unfinished form of broom. It is made up of coconut tree leaves and raw coconut sticks, specifically designed to clean rough surfaces like roads and spaces between stones. The sturdiness of the brooms helps it find usage in the industrial sector as well as household usage.

SABAI CRAFTS

Sabai also known as Natural Plastics is a weaving wonder strainght from the heart of rural Odisha, is indeed just a form of dried grass which is boiled and sun dried until it turns ochre, following which the craftsmen plait and weave the strands to create master pieces. Women bags, table mats, floor mats and many other decorative and utility items are made and marketed.

HORN CRAFT

Yet another wonder from rural Odisha, carved out of dead animal horns, this art ages back to centuries with transition in products now reaching out to pen-stands, combs, ornaments, flower vases and others. The products are carved out of the solid part of horns after soaking it in water and then heating it to a specific temperature; it is smoothened with a sharp steel blade and further pulished with charcoal ass and smeared with oil to give it sheen.
